INTRODUCTION — after existing rheology/functional-learning context
Small-data viscosity prediction already provides a useful methodological precedent: model selection, performance estimation and physical interpolation behavior require separate assessment [CITE_AVULA]. In the present task, the input is a partially observed flow curve and the output is a joint distribution over unmeasured rates. We therefore ask whether two fixed, rheologically motivated summaries can support a useful shared population correction while retaining variation outside those summaries. The intended application is continuation of hydrogel precursor-ink curves within a declared state and rate range.

INTRODUCTION — contribution and closest methodological context
Reduced representations can improve a scientific regression task, but their value should be established against unsupervised and random alternatives [CITE_CERSONSKY]. Here, Gaussian conditioning and inducing-feature constructions are established ingredients [existing19–23]. Connections between quadratic curvature and Gaussian uncertainty also have clear precedents [CITE_BIGI]. The contribution is the task-specific allocation of a shared correction to finite-scale departures from affine log–log behavior, combined with a complete functional reference residual and matched empirical controls. The formulation does not introduce a new Gaussian probability theory.

METHOD COMPARISON — refinement applicable irrespective of new outcomes
Parameter count, information access and optimization constraints describe different aspects of a fair comparison. The Gaussian controls share the correction dimension, objective, training curves, observation layouts and optimizer. We also record their parameter bounds and boundary solutions, since optimization geometry can affect GP inference [CITE_NIROOMAND]. Equal parameter counts alone do not establish equivalence of the admissible distributions. In particular, rotating a two-dimensional code preserves the unrestricted Gaussian family but generally changes a family constrained by coordinate-wise mean and Cholesky bounds. A within-subspace rotation therefore probes a different question from replacing the subspace itself.

LIMITATIONS — external stress and uncertainty
One-sided continuation along shear rate and transfer between material sources are distinct prediction challenges [CITE_SHMUEL]. The separately sourced curves provide stress tests under documented differences in composition, state and reporting; they do not establish new-batch generalization within the original precursor-ink domain. Coherent joint propagation also does not establish calibrated marginal intervals: global uncertainty summaries can conceal local failures [CITE_KELLNER]. We therefore retain the observed source-specific losses, subgroup undercoverage and sensitivity to source weighting. Stronger calibration, batch-transfer or measurement-saving claims require their own evidence and remain outside the conclusions supported here.
